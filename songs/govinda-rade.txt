=== Meaning
What is new here:
We have seen Govinda/Gopala throughout this conversation. We have seen Radhe in the Sri Rade Rade kirtan. But this chant does something specific and beautiful — it places them in direct alternation, breath by breath:
Govinda — RadheGopala — RadheGovinda — Radhe Radhe — Om

The pairing:

But in Gaudiya Vaishnavism this hierarchy quietly dissolves — Radha is not below Krishna but his very Shakti, the energy without which he cannot even perceive himself. The theologian Rupa Goswami described it thus:
Radha is Krishna's own self, expanded as the embodiment of his bliss
So calling them alternately is not calling master and servant — it is calling two aspects of one reality, like calling light and warmth — inseparable, each incomplete without the other.

"Rade / Raade"
The elongated "Raade" — with its extended vowel — is characteristic of devotional longing in kirtan. The voice stretching on her name is itself the gesture of reaching, of viraha — the sweet ache of love. You cannot say Raaade quickly. The elongation forces the heart open.
In Vrindavan, the greeting between devotees is simply "Radhe Radhe" — not namaste, not hello, but her name. Because in that tradition, Radha's name on the lips is the highest greeting, the complete philosophy, the entire path compressed into two syllables.

"Om" at the close:
The mantra ends with Om — unusual for a kirtan that has been so personal and intimate throughout. This single Om at the end does something profound:
It takes the intimate love-call of Govinda Radhe and opens it into the infinite — as if to say: this love between Radha and Krishna is not a private matter but the very sound of the universe. Their love IS Om. Om IS their love.
The structure:

The kirtan has a call and response quality built into its very structure — Govinda calls, Radhe answers. Gopala calls, Radhe answers. It enacts the eternal dynamic of the Radha-Krishna lila — divine call and devotional response, again and again, never exhausted.

Overall meaning:
"O Govinda — O Radha, Radha — O Gopala — O Radha, Radha — O Govinda — Radha, Radha — Om."

The deepest picture:
Looking at this kirtan alongside all the Vaishnava chants in this conversation, a complete picture emerges of how Krishna is approached from every direction:

This final Radha-Krishna kirtan is the most stripped down of all — no theology, no cosmic titles, no philosophy. Just two names, breathing together, ending in Om.
It is the entire Vaishnava path in one breath:
Call the beloved.Let devotion answer.Dissolve into the infinite.
Govinda.Rade Rade.Om.
