=== Meaning
A powerful shift in energy — this kirtan invokes Kali, the most fierce, most radical, and in many ways most profound of all the Hindu goddesses. After the sweetness of Rama and Hanuman, we enter the dark, transformative fire of the Mother.
Overall meaning:
"Glory to the sacred Mother — Kali, Kali, Mother — glory to the sacred Mother — O blissful Mother — Durga Devi — Mother of the Universe — sacred Mother."

The deepest picture:
Kali is the goddess who cannot be approached halfway. She demands total surrender — not the sweet surrender of Radha-Krishna bhakti, but the surrender of everything, including the desire for a comfortable spiritual experience.
She stands on the cremation ground — the place where all that is temporary is burned away. She is worshipped at midnight — when all reassuring daylight is gone. Her devotees throughout history — Ramakrishna, Ramprasad Sen, Kabiraj — all described the same experience: terror that becomes love, darkness that becomes light, death that becomes liberation.
The great Bengali poet Ramprasad Sen wrote to her like a furious, heartbroken child — scolding her, weeping to her, demanding she appear — and she did. His songs are among the most extraordinary devotional poetry ever written.
This kirtan captures that same movement — beginning with Jay Sri Ma (reverent) moving through Kali (fierce) to Ananda (blissful) to Jagadambe (cosmic) and back to Sri Ma (intimate) — the full circle of what Kali is:

She is the one who destroys everything that is not real — and what remains after that destruction is exactly what the Pavamana Mantra prays for:
Sat. Jyotir. Amritam.Reality. Light. Immortality.
Kali is the fire that gets you there.
Breaking it down:
"Jay Sri Ma"
Jay/Jai = glory, victory, praise
Sri = sacred, auspicious, radiant
Ma = Mother — the most intimate possible address to the divine
"Glory to the sacred Mother"
The simplicity of just Ma is devastating in its intimacy — not a theological title but a child's cry
"Kali"
From kala = time, darkness, black
"She who is time" / "She who is beyond time" / "The black one"
Her darkness is not evil but the darkness before creation — the infinite void from which all emerges and into which all returns
She is simultaneously the most terrifying and most loving face of the divine Mother
Her iconography tells her complete story:

"Kali Ma"
Simply "Mother Kali" — the terrifying one addressed as Mother
This juxtaposition is the heart of Kali devotion — she who appears most frightening is most completely the Mother
The great saint Ramakrishna Paramahamsa wept for her like a child for his mother, and she appeared to him directly. He said she was "made of pure consciousness"

"Ananda Ma"
Ananda = bliss, pure joy, the ground of consciousness
"O Mother of Bliss" / "Blissful Mother"
This is profound — Kali, the fierce one, is addressed as bliss
In Tantra, Kali is Sat-Chit-Ananda — being, consciousness, bliss — in her most primal form
Also echoes Anandamayi Ma — the great 20th century Bengali saint who embodied divine bliss
"Durga Devi"
Already explored — the invincible, protective Mother
Here she appears alongside Kali — two faces of the same fierce feminine:

Durga defeats the demon with strategy and power — Kali defeats it by consuming it entirely
"Jagadambe"
Already explored — "Mother of the Universe"
Jagat (world) + Amba (mother)
Here it lands differently — after Kali's darkness and Durga's fieriness, Jagadambe opens everything back to the cosmic scale — this is not just a terrifying goddess but the mother of all existence
"Sri Ma"
Returning to the opening — "Sacred Mother"
The kirtan closes where it began — a circle, like the cycle of creation and dissolution that Kali herself embodies
