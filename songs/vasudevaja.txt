=== Meaning
Overall meaning:
"Om — I surrender, releasing all that is mine — to the fully divine, the blessed one — to Vasudeva, the light that dwells in all things."

The deepest layer:
This mantra appears in the Bhagavata Purana — one of the most beloved Hindu scriptures — as the mantra given by the sage Narada to Dhruva, a young boy who went into the forest seeking God. It is said to contain within it the essence of the entire Bhagavata Purana — all twelve books, eighteen thousand verses — compressed into twelve syllables.
It is simultaneously:
A cosmic declaration — Vasudeva is everything
A personal surrender — not mine, yours
A direct encounter — calling the beloved by name, again and again, until only the name remains
This is the Dvadasakshari Mantra — the twelve-syllable mantra, one of the most sacred in all of Vaishnavism:
Om — Na — mo — Bha — ga — va — te — Va — su — de — va — ya
(count them — exactly twelve)
The number twelve itself is sacred — twelve months, twelve suns, twelve zodiac signs — suggesting cosmic completeness.
