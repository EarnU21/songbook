=== Meaning
Overall meaning:
"Om — Ganesha — Om — Ganapati — Om — Ganapati Om Ganapati Om Ganapati Om Ganapati Om..."
Until the words lose their edges and there is only the vibration — which was always the point.
The structure:
This kirtan is almost entirely name and sound — virtually no additional words. Just:
Om — the universe
Ganesha — the deity by his personal name
Ganapati — the deity by his title
Repeated, interwoven, cascading
This is the practice of nama japa taken to its purest form — the name itself is the entire practice. Nothing else is needed.

The repetition pattern:

The fourfold repetition of Ganapati Om is particularly powerful — four being the number of stability and foundation, the four directions, the four states of consciousness (jagrat, svapna, sushupti, turiya — waking, dreaming, deep sleep, pure awareness). Chanting four times in each round is like planting the name in every corner of existence.

Om and Ganesha — their deep connection:
This kirtan reveals something profound — Ganesha and Om are essentially the same:
Ganesha's elephant shape is said to be the visual form of Om — his trunk the curve, his body the roundness, his tail the tail of the Sanskrit character ॐ
Om is the primordial sound of creation — Ganesha is the lord of all beginnings
Om pervades everything — Ganesha governs everything
To chant Om Ganesha Om is therefore almost a tautology of the sacred — the sound of the universe calling the lord of the universe by name

Ganapati Om — the rhythm:
What makes this kirtan so distinctive is its rhythm — Ganapati Om has a natural lilt:
GA-na-pa-ti OM — long, short, short, short, LONG
This creates an almost drumbeat quality — fitting for Ganesha, who is associated with the mridanga (clay drum) and with rhythm itself as the organizing principle of creation. The Om landing at the end of each phrase is like the downbeat, the ground, everything resolving and immediately beginning again.
The deepest picture:
There is a teaching in the Ganapatya tradition (the Shaiva sect devoted specifically to Ganesha as supreme deity) that the entire universe is Ganesha's dance — every atom vibrating with his energy, every beginning and ending his breath.
This kirtan enacts that teaching physically — as the repetitions multiply, as Ganapati Om cascades four times, eight times, again and again, something happens in the body and mind of the singer. The analytical mind that separates — that says this word means this, that word means that — begins to quiet. What remains is simply the vibration of the name, which the tradition says is Ganesha himself.
This is why Ganesha kirtans often go longest in seva (devotional music sessions) — because once the cascade of Ganapati Om begins, it becomes self-sustaining, like a wheel that no longer needs to be pushed.
The name chants itself.
The devotee disappears.
Only Ganapati Om remains —
and that, the tradition says, was always all there was.
Om Ganesha. Om Ganapati. Om.
