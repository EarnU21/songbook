=== Meaning
This is a devotional chant to Shiva, one of the principal deities of Hinduism — the destroyer and transformer in the Hindu Trinity.
Overall meaning:
"O Shankara, bringer of peace — O compassionate one — Lord of the Universe — Supreme Lord"
It is a chant of pure adoration, stringing together four of Shiva's most beautiful names in a flowing, rhythmic salutation. Each name reveals a different facet of him — as peace-giver, as compassionate father, as cosmic ruler, as supreme reality.
It moves beautifully from the intimate (Shankara — the personal, loving Shiva) to the vast (Parameshvara — the infinite, absolute Shiva).

Breaking it down:
"Shankara"
One of Shiva's most beloved names
From sham (peace, bliss, auspiciousness) + kara (maker, one who brings)
"The one who brings peace and auspiciousness"
Also interpreted as "the beneficent one"
"Karunakara"
Karuna = compassion, mercy + kara = maker, bestower
"The one who is the source of compassion"
Despite Shiva's fierce reputation, this name emphasizes his boundless mercy and grace toward devotees
"Jagadeswara"
Jagat = the world/universe + Ishwara = Lord, master
"Lord of the Universe"
A majestic title recognizing Shiva's sovereignty over all creation
"Parameshvara"
Parama = supreme, highest + Ishvara = Lord
"The Supreme Lord"
The ultimate title — above all others, the highest divine principle
