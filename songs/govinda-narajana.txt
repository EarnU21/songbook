=== Meaning
Overall meaning:
"O Govinda, O Narayana — O Gopala, O Narayana — O Govinda, O Gopala, O Narayana — O Hare, O Govinda, O Gopala, O Narayana."

The deepest picture:
This kirtan does something quietly profound — it holds two apparently opposite truths simultaneously without resolving the tension between them:
Narayana — the infinite, the cosmic, the one who contains all worlds, who rests on the primordial ocean, before whom even Brahma bows
Gopala — the small boy herding cows barefoot through the mud of Vrindavan, stealing butter, playing his flute, running to Radha
In most spiritual traditions the infinite and the intimate are kept separate — the cosmic God is worshipped with awe and distance, the personal God with warmth and closeness. But Vaishnavism insists they are the same being — and that the highest devotion holds both at once:
The infinite ocean of consciousness
is also
the boy who steals your butter and laughs.
To feel that fully — the vastness and the tenderness simultaneously — is said to be the very heart of prema bhakti, pure devotional love.
This small, gentle kirtan in D minor carries that entire mystery in four names and three chords.
Hare Govinda. Gopala. Narayana.
Breaking it down:
"Govinda"
Already explored — "protector of cows, one who delights the senses, finder of the earth"
But here in Dm it carries a different quality than before — darker, more longing, more aching
The same name that in a major key feels playful here feels like yearning
"Narayana" (here rendered "Narajana" — a phonetic/folk variation)
One of Vishnu/Krishna's most ancient and cosmic names
Nara = human being, waters, the primordial waters + Ayana = dwelling, refuge, path
Multiple simultaneous meanings:
Nara also means the individual soul — so Narayana = "he in whom all souls dwell and from whom all souls emerge"
This is Vishnu/Krishna at his most vast and cosmic — the infinite ocean of consciousness upon which the entire universe floats like a lotus
"Gopala"
Already explored — "tender of cows, nurturer"
Krishna as the intimate cowherd boy of Vrindavan
Where Narayana is the cosmic ocean, Gopala is the beloved child playing on its shore
"Hare"
From Hari — one of Vishnu/Krishna's most beloved names
Hri = to steal, to captivate, to carry away
"The one who steals the heart" — Krishna as the irresistible thief of love
Also understood as "the one who removes sin and suffering"
Hare is the vocative form — "O Hari, O heart-stealer"
The same Hare that opens the Maha Mantra: Hare Krishna Hare Krishna / Krishna Krishna Hare Hare / Hare Rama Hare Rama...

The movement of names:
What is extraordinarily beautiful in this kirtan is how it weaves three dimensions of the same divine being:

The kirtan keeps bringing these together — the infinite and the intimate side by side, the cosmic ocean and the cowherd boy, the one who contains the universe and the one who steals your heart — because in the Vaishnava understanding these are not different beings but different faces of one love.
