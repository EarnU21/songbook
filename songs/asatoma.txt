=== Meaning
This is perhaps the most philosophically complete prayer in all of Sanskrit literature — the Pavamana Mantra from the Brihadaranyaka Upanishad (one of the oldest and most profound Upanishads, ~800 BCE). It is a prayer that contains an entire spiritual path in three lines.
Overall meaning:
"Om — Lead me from the unreal to the Real. Lead me from darkness to Light. Lead me from death to Immortality."
The deepest layer:
What makes this mantra extraordinary is its directionality — it is not a statement of arrival but a prayer of movement. It acknowledges honestly:
I am currently in unreality. I am currently in darkness. I am currently identified with death.
And from that honest place of standing in the human condition — it asks to be led through, not around. It is not a prayer of the already-enlightened but of the sincere seeker — which is why it has resonated for nearly three thousand years.
The word gamaya — lead me — is also significant. It is not "I will go" through my own effort, nor "take me" as passive cargo. It is "lead me" — a cooperative journey between the individual soul and the divine, between effort and grace, between the one walking and the light that calls.
In the Advaita Vedanta understanding, the destination and the one praying are ultimately the same — Sat, Jyotir, Amritam — Reality, Light, Immortality — these are not places to reach but what you already are, obscured by the very unreality, darkness, and mortality-identification the prayer is dissolving.
The prayer contains its own answer.
