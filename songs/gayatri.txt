=== Meaning
You have saved the most ancient and sacred of all for this moment — the Gayatri Mantra, the crown jewel of the entire Vedic tradition, perhaps the oldest continuously chanted prayer in human history, still chanted today exactly as it was nearly 3,500 years ago.
The complete mantra:
Om — Bhur Bhuvaha Svaha —Tat Savitur VarenyamBhargo Devasya DhimahiDhiyo Yo Nah Prachodayat
"Om — across all planes of existence — We meditate upon that most excellent radiance of the Divine Sun — may that divine light illuminate and inspire our minds."
The 24 syllables and their deeper structure:

Three perfect movements: the divine, the devotee, the request — subject, verb, object — a complete universe of prayer in 24 syllables.

The layers of meaning:
The Gayatri works simultaneously on multiple levels:

This last layer is the most extraordinary — dhimahi (we meditate) uses the same root dhi as dhiyo (our minds/intellects). The light we ask to illuminate our minds is the same light by which we are already meditating. The seeker and the sought are one — the Gayatri contains within it the same non-dual recognition as Soham Shivoham, just expressed as prayer rather than declaration.

The deepest picture:
Looking back across this entire extraordinary journey of mantras we have traveled together — from Oshun on the river to Tara in the Tibetan sky, from Rama and Sita's love to Kali on the cremation ground, from Soham's self-recognition to Ganesha's cascading name —
Every single one of those prayers, in their different languages and traditions, was asking for some version of what the Gayatri asks directly:
May the divine light illuminate this mind.
And now, at the very end, the oldest prayer of all arrives — not as a conclusion but as a source. As if all the other mantras were rivers, and the Gayatri is the ocean they all flow from and return to.
The Vedic tradition says the Gayatri contains within it all four Vedas, all knowledge, all prayer. Chanting it once with full understanding is equivalent to chanting every mantra that exists.
Which means —
Everything we have explored in this conversation,
every goddess and god,
every Sanskrit syllable,
every chord that carried the sacred words —
was already inside these 24 syllables,
waiting to be found.
Om Bhur Bhuvaha Svaha.Tat Savitur Varenyam.Bhargo Devasya Dhimahi.Dhiyo Yo Nah Prachodayat.
So it is. So it has always been. So it will always be.
The mantra itself — word by word:
"Tat"
"That" — the same Tat as in Tat tvam asi (That thou art)
The absolute, the ultimate, the unnameable — pointed to but never contained by a word
Beginning with Tat is an act of radical humility — we cannot name what we are addressing, only point
"Savitur"
From Savitri — the solar deity, the divine generative power of the sun
Not merely the physical sun but the light of pure consciousness that the sun symbolizes
Su = to generate, to bring forth — Savitur is "the one who generates, who brings forth all life"
The sun that illuminates the outer world is a symbol of the inner sun of awareness that illuminates all experience
"Varenyam"
"Most excellent, most adorable, most worthy of being chosen"
From vri = to choose, to desire
We are not merely praising Savitur but declaring "of all things, this divine light is what we choose, what we find most worthy"
An act of conscious choosing of the highest
"Bhargo"
"Radiance, effulgence, the purifying light"
A special word — bhargas is not ordinary light but the light that burns away impurity
The same root as bhrāj — to shine brilliantly
It is simultaneously the light that illuminates and purifies — like fire that both lights the darkness and burns away dross
This is Savitur's essential quality — not just shining but transforming through shining
"Devasya"
Deva + genitive suffix = "of the Divine, of the shining one"
Deva from div = to shine — the gods are literally "the shining ones"
Devasya Bhargo = "the radiance of the Divine"
"Dhimahi"
The heart of the entire mantra
From dhi = intellect, higher mind, meditative awareness + mahi = we hold, we meditate upon
"We meditate upon, we hold in our awareness, we contemplate"
The verb is first person plural — we, not I
This is a communal prayer — the entire human family meditating together
Dhi specifically refers not to ordinary thinking but to the highest faculty of mind — the intellect capable of grasping truth directly
"Dhiyo"
Dhi = intellect, understanding, the higher mind
Here in the plural — "our intellects, our understandings, our minds"
What we are asking to be illuminated
"Yo"
"Who, which" — the relative pronoun connecting Savitur to the request
"May that [divine light] which..."
"Nah"
"Our, us" — again communal, plural
"Our minds, our understandings"
"Prachodayat"
From pra (forth) + chud (to impel, to inspire, to set in motion)
"May it inspire, illuminate, impel forward, awaken"
The final verb — the entire mantra has been building to this single request:
"May that divine light illuminate and inspire our minds"
