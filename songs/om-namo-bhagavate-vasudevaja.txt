=== Meaning
This is a Vaishnava mantra and kirtan — a devotional chant from the Hindu tradition, specifically honoring Vasudeva (Krishna/Vishnu) and the Guru.

Breaking it down:
"Om Namo Bhagavate Vasudevaya"
This is actually one of the most sacred Vaishnava mantras, known as the Dvadasakshari (12-syllable mantra):
"Om" — the primordial sound, the universal vibration
"Namo" — I bow, I honor, I surrender (namas = reverence)
"Bhagavate" — to the Blessed One, the Divine, the Lord (Bhagavan = the one who possesses all divine qualities)
"Vasudevaya" — to Vasudeva, a name of Krishna (son of Vasudeva) and also of Vishnu, meaning "the one who dwells in all things" (vasu = light/dwelling + deva = divine)
So: "Om, I bow to the Divine Lord Vasudeva (Krishna/Vishnu)"

"Guru Deva Nama Om"
"Guru" — the teacher, the one who leads from darkness to light (gu = darkness, ru = remover)
"Deva" — divine, shining one
"Nama" — shortened form of namas, a bow of reverence
"Om" — the universal sound, closing the phrase
So: "I bow to the divine Guru, Om"

The chords (Dm – F – Am – Dm / Gm – Bb – F – C)
A beautifully melancholic D minor tonal center, moving through its relative major colors. The Gm–Bb–F–C section lifts toward the relative major, giving the "Guru Deva" part a feeling of opening up or ascending — very fitting for honoring the teacher.

Overall meaning and spirit:
The chant weaves together two devotions — surrender to the Divine in all things (Vasudeva/Krishna), and gratitude to the Guru who points the way. It's a chant of humility, love, and recognition that the divine is everywhere and the teacher is its messenger.
