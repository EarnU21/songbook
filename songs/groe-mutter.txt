=== Meaning
Overall meaning:
"Durga, Durga, Durga — glory, glory, Mother — glory to the blissful Mother."
The deepest picture:
Across this entire conversation we have encountered the divine Mother in many forms:

And yet this kirtan, the simplest Durga chant of all, reveals something all the elaborate theology cannot:
The Mother does not need to be described to be present.
Call her name.
Call it again.
Call it a third time.
Durga.Durga.Durga.
She comes.
Not because the theology was correct,
not because the Sanskrit was perfect,
not because the philosophy was understood —
but because a child called for its mother.
And mothers come.
Jai Jai Ma.Jai Ananda Ma.
What strikes immediately:
Every other Durga chant in this conversation surrounded her name with epithets — Jagadambe, Devi, Mahalakshmi. Here there is almost nothing but her name. Just:
Durga. Durga. Durga.
This is the practice of nama sankirtana at its most radical — the belief that the name itself is the deity, that repetition of the name alone is complete, that nothing else is needed. No description. No theology. Just the direct call.

"Durga" — revisited more deeply:
We have met her before in this conversation, but here her name deserves deeper attention since it carries the entire weight of the kirtan:
From durg = fortress, that which is difficult to access, that which is hard to overcome
"She who is the invincible fortress" — both the protected place AND the protection itself
Also parsed as: Du + r + ga — three seed syllables combined, each carrying protective energy
Her name is simultaneously what she is (invincible) and what she gives (invincibility to her devotees)
When you call Durga Durga Durga three times in succession you are not merely repeating — you are intensifying, like knocking on a door with increasing urgency, or like a fire catching — spark, flame, blaze.

"Jai Jai Ma"
Jai Jai — doubled glory, doubled victory
Ma — Mother, the most intimate address
The doubling of Jai after the triple Durga creates a rhythmic release — "Durga Durga Durga — GLORY GLORY MOTHER" — tension and release, invocation and celebration
