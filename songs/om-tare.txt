=== Meaning
Overall meaning:
"Om — O Tara, who liberates, who protects from fear, who swiftly frees — so be it!"
It is traditionally chanted for protection, healing, overcoming obstacles, and swift help in difficult times. Tara is said to respond immediately to sincere calls — her compassion is described as instantaneous, like a mother responding to her child.
This is the Green Tara mantra, one of the most beloved mantras in Tibetan Buddhism — a prayer to Tara, the female Bodhisattva of compassion, protection, and swift action.

Breaking it down:
"Om" — the universal primordial sound, opening and consecrating the mantra
"Tare" — "she who liberates" — from the Sanskrit root tṛ (to cross over, to save). Tara is the one who ferries beings across the ocean of suffering. This syllable invokes her as liberator from worldly suffering
"Tuttare" — "she who liberates from fears" — a more intense form, invoking protection from the eight great fears (pride, ignorance, hatred, envy, wrong views, greed, attachment, doubt). She liberates from both outer and inner dangers
"Ture" — "she who liberates from illness and duality" — the swiftest, most immediate liberation, cutting through the root of suffering itself. Also connected to swift action, which is Tara's signature quality
"Svaha" (spelled here Suaha) — a Vedic exclamation meaning "so be it," "hail," "may it be established" — like a devotional "amen," sealing the mantra and offering it to the deity

The three liberations:
The progression Tare → Tuttare → Ture is understood as three layers:
Liberation from samsara (the cycle of suffering)
Liberation from fear and danger
Liberation from ignorance at the deepest level
